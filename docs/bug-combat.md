# Barn care and combat

A Barn starts with three stalls. Each holds an egg, a living bug or an unburied memorial. One bug is the partner: the one in My House's terrarium and beside its owner in the mine. My House shows that same bug; it does not create another one or provide passive care. The server owns needs, clocks, progression and fight results.

## XP and care

- **XP fades with level.** Care, item, daily, feeder, toy and losing XP are whole through level 5, then fall in a straight line to a sixth at level 10 and beyond (`xpFade` in `server/engine/bug-combat.js`): a care action gives 9 XP up to level 5, then 8, 6, 5, 3 and 2 from level 10. The amounts below are the whole ones.
- **Win XP follows the level beaten** (`gapXp`): the base and upset bonus are multiplied by 1.25 for one level above the winner, 1.5 for two, up to 2 for four or more; and by 0.6, 0.3 and 0.1 for one, two, and three or more levels below. A loss or a draw pays the base, faded by the bug's own level.
- Free care in the Barn or My House and store food give 9 XP each time. There is no daily cap: the care and item cooldowns are the only limit.
- At each 24-hour checkpoint since hatching, a living bug earns 8 XP if average needs are at least 25. The toy that is out adds 2 to 15 XP at the partner's checkpoint whatever its needs (a stalled bug has no toy and earns none), and an active feeder adds 9; neither gives immediate XP. Accrual settles when the server next refreshes the pet, using the toy that is out at that moment.
- A paid bug item does something free care cannot. Store food feeds like the free Feed and also packs a lunch worth 2 to 10 HP: the bug eats it in its next fight, in the Barn or the mine, the first time it is left standing below half health. Eating costs no turn. It carries one lunch, keeps the bigger, and keeps an uneaten one for the fight after.
- Food resets the starvation clock. An automatic feeder fills Fed, Mood and Clean, keeps all three full and pauses starvation while active, so the bug holds full condition. A feeder use adds 14 days, up to 60 days ahead. Toys do not protect against starvation.
- The Auto-Mister, Cosy Heat Lamp and Golden Ambrosia are no longer sold, because each did less than a free button or the feeder. One already held still shows in its owner's store and still works: 40 Clean or Mood and 6 XP, or a full meal, a 10 HP lunch and seven feeder days.
- By default, death occurs after 21 days without feeding, or 21 days after feeder expiry, with the server's minimum-life grace period. An empty Fed meter alone does not kill a bug. Visiting or reopening the room does not reset these clocks. A memorial occupies the Barn until buried.
- Buying a consumable does not apply it; **Use** spends it. Toys and gear are permanent equipment. Toys improve active Play and earn daily XP rather than supplying passive Mood; looks and battle gear are worn by one bug at a time: a piece bought for one bug is not on the others, so a second bug needs its own copy (up to one per possible stall) or the piece moved across, which takes it off the first. The store asks which bug when you keep more than one. Battle gear comes in two kinds, and a bug wears one of each: an attack item adds at most 6% to the damage of its hits, a defense item takes at most 6% off each hit it receives (venom and trait damage are not hits), and both are drawn on the bug. Gear equipped before the split moved to its own slot the first time the server started, and whatever a player's bug had on before gear became per-bug went to their partner. Item-specific amounts and cooldowns are shown in the shop and owned by `server/catalog/bug.js` and `server/shop.js`.

## Stalls and the partner

- **Stalls.** Three to start. The fourth, fifth and sixth cost $1,500, $2,000 and $2,000 (`STALL_COSTS` in `server/barn.js`), bought from the last slot in the Barn's stall row. An egg, a bug and a memorial not yet laid to rest each take one.
- **Wild bugs.** A bug jarred in the mine takes a free stall at full health, at its own level or two above the bug that knocked it out, whichever is lower. See [Acid Mines](acid-mines.md).
- **Eggs.** An empty Barn gives you a 15% chance to find an egg each new shift. Any occupied stall takes you out of the roll, including an unhatched egg or an unburied memorial. An egg is always one of the common species. Catch wild bugs to fill additional stalls.
- **The partner.** The first bug is the partner; **Make partner** on any other hatched, living bug swaps them, except while one of your bugs is in a fight. The mine guide, mine battles, My House and store items (food, feeders, misters, toys) all act on the partner.
- **A stalled bug lives five times slower** (`BENCH_SLOW`). Its needs decay at a fifth of the rate, it starves after 105 days instead of 21, and its 24-hour XP checkpoint comes every five days. It still needs looking after: care works on any bug, on the usual cooldowns, and gives the usual 12 XP. Swapping partners carries each bug's needs over as they are. A feeder's dates stay on the wall clock, so one bought for a bug that is later stalled covers fewer of its slow days.
- **No empty partner's place.** When the partner is laid to rest or released, the first living stalled bug becomes the partner.
- **Releasing.** Release lets a living bug go (not while one of your bugs is fighting). It leaves the Barn at once and becomes a stray in the mines, with its name, level and owner's name: see [Acid Mines](acid-mines.md). Its gear and packed lunch stay behind. `barn.json` keeps up to 50 uncaught strays in `strays`; the oldest wander off for good.
- **Fights.** Any standing bug can post, join or be called out; the ring's roster lists every living bug, and a challenge names both. An owner has one fight at a time.
- **The Bug Center** treats knocked-out bugs from any stall. The nurse charges $50 for immediate treatment and treats the bug for free an hour after the knockout. With two or more bugs knocked out she sees them all at once: one question with the whole bill on the button (free for every bug that has rested an hour, $50 for each one that has not), one tap heals them all, and the money goes out as one `barn-heal` ledger line (`POST /api/barn/heal-all`). Short of money, she says what everyone costs and what you have and offers to heal as many as that covers (the partner first, then the longest wait). Sick or collected bugs still need the specialist, and nothing heals just by opening the page.

### Parked for later: one-of-a-kind bugs

Left out on purpose, as a possible expansion (rare species, wild bugs and the jar are in: see Types below and [Acid Mines](acid-mines.md)): a legendary species of which only one lives in the whole Academy at a time. Whoever jars it owns the only one, and it is up for grabs again when that owner releases or loses it.

## Leveling and moves

Any hatched bug may bout, from **level 1**, and any bug still standing at the end recovers to full HP immediately, even after a loss on points. A bug reduced to 0 HP needs the Bug Center. Challenge another player's bug, post a bout open to anyone, or join one; there is no automatic opponent or level matching. Both bugs earn **15 XP** and the winner a bonus on top that follows the line (below): 8 in an even fight, up to 14 for the longest shot and as little as 2 for a sure thing. Both records update. A bug guiding its miner in the Acid Mines earns **2 XP** for each pool with acid it finds. Watching a replay gives no XP or money. Five wins earn the title **Barn Menace**.

| Level | Total XP | Unlock |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 1 | 0 | Basic attack, technique and bouts |
| 4 | 360 | Species special |
| 7 | 1,440 | Alternative special |
| 10 | 3,240 | Capstone |

The XP curve is `40 * (level - 1)^2`. **A bug holds at level 9 until it evolves** (`PAUSED_AT` in `server/engine/creatures.js`): XP keeps counting and is kept, and wild bugs roll no higher than 9. A bug's stage sets its ceiling (`STAGE_CAPS`): 9 as hatched, 23 once evolved, 30 after a second evolution that is not built yet. Circuit ribbons no longer move it. The ceiling is stamped on each bug as `cap` when it is refreshed; XP can still accumulate past it and counts once it rises. Each level after the first adds 5 HP and 3% base attack damage (`GROWTH` in `server/engine/bug-combat.js`), so a level 10 has 145 HP and hits 27% harder than a level 1. With both bugs left to themselves, one level is worth about 60/40 and two about 80/20. Fight XP affects subsequent bouts, not the bout awarding it.

Every fight, in the Barn or the mine, is the same turn-based fight in the ring. The bugs act in turn, one action every few seconds. Under the ring, a panel shows what your bug will do next; tap its moves to line up as many as three, and tap a lined-up move to take it back. Each move shows its chance to hit and the damage it would do right now (or the HP it would heal); the line under the buttons spells out the move you last touched, with its poison, guard or weakening and how long it rests after. A bug with nothing lined up, or whose lined-up move is still recovering, picks for itself, so a fight never waits on an owner: one who is away simply gets their bug's own picks, and a fight nobody is watching runs straight to its end. The saved `balanced`, `aggressive` or `defensive` stance steers those picks (a balanced bug mends when hurt and otherwise takes its specials in turn) and still shifts damage dealt and taken. Specials have cooldowns in the user's own turns. Each species also has one trait, drawn from a fact on its sheet (the Combat tab and the panel name it; `TRAIT` in `server/engine/bug-combat.js` holds the numbers). The Combat tab describes effects; move names grant no hidden bonuses, and the fighting styles in replays (hoppers, chargers, rollers and so on) are animation only. A bug's type is a rule: see Types below. Condition uses the lowest care need, and gear bonuses of the same kind do not stack: one attack item and one defense item count. Initiative, accuracy and damage vary using server-seeded randomness. Piercing level-10 capstones have a 5% chance on a nonlethal hit to leave the target at 1 HP; an ordinary knockout stays a knockout. Criticals are recorded in the replay.

## Evolution

A bug evolves once, by accident, and its owner is never asked. The constants are in `server/barn.js`.

- **Due.** The partner is due when its XP reaches what level 10 needs (`EVOLVE_FROM`), though it still shows level 9. Stalled bugs are never due.
- **Purple acid.** Every shift has the day's purple pool (`purplePool`, see [Acid Mines](acid-mines.md)), and nobody can mine it. Each mine clock-in with a due partner rolls for a purple shift (`purpleChance`): one in four at the XP for level 10, half at 11, three in four at 12, certain from 13 (`EVOLVE_BY`). The roll uses the Barn's own random source (`evoInt`), never the ones that decide pay. A purple shift carries `purple: { afterMs }`: the bug walks into the pool and drinks when its miner passes within two tiles, or `afterMs` in (45 to 90 seconds) wherever they are, comes back to its miner at 40% of its pace, falls over, and the browser clocks out (8 seconds in all). On any other shift the bug will not go near it and the ticker says so once.
- **Sick.** However a purple shift ends, the partner goes home sick (`fallSick`): the shift's receipt carries `sick` (its name), acid already collected is paid as usual, and the Foreman does not lecture. A sick bug is out like a fainted one, cannot be cared for, and does not recover by resting.
- **The Bug Center.** `POST /api/barn/heal` on a sick bug evolves it at once (`evolve`). It takes half the owner's cash (`BILL_SHARE`, rounded down to the cent; nothing from someone with nothing) as a `barn-evolve` ledger entry, and answers with `evolved: { botched, levelBefore, levelAfter, bill: { had, took, left } }`. The browser only sends that request as the last story panel is passed, so leaving earlier changes nothing.
- **Evolved.** `evo: 1` on the bug. It leaves the level 9 hold for a ceiling of 23, so the XP it was holding counts at once and the level 10 move unlocks. Its style carries `evo` for the sprite. Stage II is the species' own evolved monster (`client/src/barn/stageTwoArt.js`): a front-facing hero pose with one bold silhouette, a dominant colour of its own (`STAGE_TWO_PAL`), sharp eyes and two- or three-tone cel shading, drawn natively on a 64 or 128 grid and only ever scaled up nearest-neighbour. A botched Stage II keeps the older drawing (its own body under a porcelain face). Stage III (`evo: 2`) is a two-legged frame built for its kind, wearing its old shell as a breastplate and its wings if it has them (`client/src/barn/evolutionArt.js`), and dressed for where it is from: a hat, something worn and something in its free hand (`evolutionWear.js`). A hat or helmet from the store goes on instead of its own.
- **Botched.** One rebuild in fifteen (`BOTCH_ODDS`) is botched, for good: same bill, a porcelain face, the arm on the wrong side, and one of three quirks picked by a second roll (`QUIRKS` and `QUIRK` in `server/engine/bout.js`), saved as `botched`. **Lazy:** 8% of its turns it does nothing. **Berserk:** it hits 30% harder, is hit 18% harder, and never uses a healing move, whoever lines one up. **Haywire:** each hit rolls 0.4 to 1.9 times its power instead of 0.8 to 1.25, and 6% of its turns the arm hits its owner for 6% of its health and the turn is lost. Left to themselves against the same bug unbotched, a lazy one wins about 30% and the other two 40% to 50%. The quirk rolls are only drawn for a botched bug, so every other fight is unchanged.
- **The story panels.** The four panels are illustrations when `client/public/bugcenter/evo-1.webp` to `evo-4.webp` exist (3:2, drawn to match the nurse's portrait); until then the pixel drawings in `BugCenter.jsx` stand in.

### The second evolution

Stage two to stage three. The constants sit beside the first evolution's in `server/barn.js`.

- **He comes for it.** A stage two partner with the XP for level 23 (`MECH_FROM`) is due. Each mine clock-in rolls (`rollCollect`, the same ramp: one in four at 23, certain from 26, `MECH_BY`) and stamps `collect` on the shift. However that shift ends, the bug leaves with the specialist (`collect`): the receipt carries `taken` (its name) and the Foreman does not lecture. Nothing happens in the tunnels, so it cannot be mistaken for the Heeshee, and a bug the Heeshee holds is not collected.
- **Away.** A taken bug is out like a fainted one, cannot be cared for and does not come back by resting. Its view carries `taken` and `donors`: the owner's other hatched, living bugs that are home. A legendary is never a donor.
- **The Bug Center.** `POST /api/barn/heal` with `donor` (a bug id from `donors`) rebuilds it (`rebuild`). The donor is removed in the same write, with a line in the graveyard; no money moves. An owner with no donors sends no `donor` and pays half their cash (`BILL_SHARE`) instead. With donors and none named, the call is refused. The answer's `evolved` carries `stage: 2` and `donor`.
- **Stage three.** `evo: 2`, a ceiling of level 30.
- **Botched.** The same one in fifteen. It replaces any earlier quirk with one of `MAN_QUIRKS`: **polite** (half the time it gives up its first turn), **salaried** (its hits do 90% after its sixth turn) or **squeamish** (its hits do half against a foe under half health). Against the same bug unbotched they win roughly 25% to 45%.

Mech parts for an evolved bug are sold at his counter in the Bug Center and work in the mine: see [Acid Mines](acid-mines.md#mech-parts).

Not built yet: who the specialist is, and new story panels for the second visit (it reuses the tools and the curtain).

Bugs grow slightly through level 10, with shared size and equipped-art scaling across the Barn, room, shop and bouts. Young forms become adults at level 3 where appropriate; some species retain a similar form. These are stylized life stages, not exact biology. Replays use the historical level/form rather than today's pet.

## Types

Every species has a type, taken from its fact sheet (`server/engine/bug-types.js`). There are nine, and each beats three of the others and loses to three. Flier, Brawler, Shell, Toxic, Ambush and Dazzle are the everyday six; Fire, Sonic and Water each beat two of those and one another in a ring. Fifty-two species, five to seven in each type.

| Type | Beats | Loses to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flier | Brawler, Toxic, Fire | Ambush, Dazzle, Sonic |
| Brawler | Shell, Dazzle, Fire | Flier, Toxic, Water |
| Shell | Toxic, Dazzle, Sonic | Brawler, Ambush, Fire |
| Toxic | Ambush, Brawler, Sonic | Shell, Flier, Water |
| Ambush | Flier, Shell, Water | Toxic, Dazzle, Fire |
| Dazzle | Ambush, Flier, Water | Brawler, Shell, Sonic |
| Fire | Shell, Ambush, Sonic | Flier, Brawler, Water |
| Sonic | Flier, Dazzle, Water | Shell, Toxic, Fire |
| Water | Brawler, Toxic, Fire | Ambush, Dazzle, Sonic |

A bug's hits land 10% harder on a type it beats and 8% softer on a type that beats it (`EDGE`). Only hits: venom, a bombardier's pop, a stick insect's spray, an exploding ant's burst and healing are untouched, as with gear. Fights are close, so that is a lot. Over every pairing at equal level, with both bugs left to themselves, the type with the edge wins about four fights in five, roughly what two levels are worth, and each type's record over all 52 species stays within a few points of even (`test/bug-types.test.js`). The line counts the edge, so stakes and win XP follow it. One exception, from its trait: there is no edge either way between a fire ant and a Water bug, because fire ants raft.

Each type also gives every bug in it one perk, whatever its species or level (`PERK`):

| Type | Perk |
| --- | --- |
| Flier | Quick: attacks are 6% less likely to hit it. |
| Brawler | Heavy: every hit it lands is 5% harder. |
| Shell | Armour: takes 9% less from every hit that is not a pierce. |
| Toxic | Taint: every hit it lands poisons a target that is not poisoned already, 2 a turn for two turns. Its venom moves still do their own, stronger. |
| Ambush | First strike: its first attack cannot miss and hits 40% harder. |
| Dazzle | Startle: the first time it is left standing below half health it flashes, and the opponent's next attack does 50% less. |
| Fire | Flare-up: below half health, every hit it lands is 10% harder. |
| Sonic | Loud: a guard blocks 35% less of its hits. |
| Water | Dive: after a hit lands on it, the next attack against it is 6% less likely to hit. |

The numbers were set by fighting every pairing at levels 1, 5 and 10 until each type's record sat within about five points of even, with the edge still worth 77% to 78%. Ambush is the low one at level 10, about 45%: more of the newer species are Toxic and Dazzle, which beat it. The move panel's odds count the perks: a Toxic bug's strike shows its poison, an Ambush bug's first strike shows 100%.

A bug's type is on its card (the Barn's Card tab, with its moves, trait and what it beats), beside its name in the stall, the ring's roster and a fight, and in the mine's line when a wild bug is first seen. Each species is also Common, Uncommon or Rare (`rarityOf`). The uncommon ones are those whose own sheet says they are rare, threatened, barely known or a record holder. Eight are Rare, with one more stat point than everything else (10, not 9): the Asian Bombardier Beetle, Shrill Thorntree Cicada, Sea Skater, Diabolical Ironclad Beetle, New Zealand Glowworm, Black Beauty Stick Insect, Emerald Cockroach Wasp and Exploding Ant. An egg is always a common; uncommon and rare bugs are only met wild, and a rare only on a rough day (see [Acid Mines](acid-mines.md)).

## The ring, challenges and open bouts

- **The ring** is in the Barn for every signed-in player, with or without a bug. It lists every living bug whose owner is not hidden, the bouts open to anyone and the latest 20 fights, any of which can be replayed.
- **The line** is a bug's chance of winning, worked out by the server: the two bugs as they stand (level, health, condition, gear, packed lunch, stance) fight 400 times with both left to themselves, and the share is rounded to the nearest 5% and kept between 10% and 90% (`line` in `server/engine/bout.js`). Each seed is fought both ways round, so two identical bugs are exactly even and the two corners always add up to 100%. The board shows your chance against every bug that can fight, every open bout and every challenge; the ring names the favourite beside its level. An owner who picks the moves can beat it. It is worked out from condition, gear and stance without showing them, so it hints at what stays private.
- **Stakes follow the line.** Whoever posts a bout or sends a challenge names their own stake. The other corner puts up fair odds against it, `stake × (1 − p) / p` to the whole dollar, where `p` is the first bug's chance (`riskFor`), so a favourite risks more to win less: against a $10 stake from a 20% underdog, the favourite puts up $40. The winner takes both. The line is read again when the fight starts; if it has moved since the page showed it, joining or accepting is refused (409) and nothing is taken.
- **A challenge** names another player's bug and a stake. Both must be hatched and alive; a fainted or Heeshee-held target is refused. Nothing is held. It stands for 24 hours; the sender can withdraw it and the owner can decline it. A player can have one out per opponent, with no other limit. It lapses if either bug dies or is replaced, or once the day is up. A challenger who is already in a fight, has a bout posted, or whose bug has fainted does not lapse the challenge: the ring shows a wait and Accept stays off until they are free.
- **Accepting** takes both stakes at that moment and starts the fight, exactly as joining a posted bout does. The player who accepted or joined sees it at once; the other owner's bug fights on its own until they open the Barn, where they can take over. Both bugs must be alive, not fainted and not already in a fight, neither player may have a bout posted, the challenger must cover the stake they named and the accepter what the line asks of them. A refused accept leaves the challenge standing, and the error names the other player in the third person (never "your" for their bug). Incoming challenges carry `wait` when Accept cannot work yet.
- The Barn button shows a count, checked once a minute while the page is showing: challenges waiting on you, bouts other players have open (only while your bug is fit to fight), one while your bug is in the ring, and one for a fight that was decided while you were away. Opening the Barn plays that fight and clears it. A player with Slack alerts on also gets one message from Guntrad when challenged, naming the challenger, both bugs and the stake; it is not sent again if the same challenge is repeated, and a Slack failure never fails the challenge.
- A stake is **$0–$10,000 in play money**, in steps of $10 (`BOUT_STAKE_STEP`); the Barn's stake box opens on $100, or the most its owner can cover. Bout money never has cents. Only the stake a player names is capped; what the line asks of the other corner can be up to nine times it. Posting holds the host's stake. Joining pays what the line asks and starts the fight; both stakes are held until it ends, when the winner receives the whole pot. A draw refunds both stakes. A server restart during a fight calls it off: both stakes are returned and nobody earns XP or a record. There are no spectator bets or house fees.
- One listing per player. The host can cancel for a full refund. Listings expire after one hour and are refunded when the Barn next checks them; a missing, dead or replaced host bug also invalidates the listing. Posting and cancelling award no XP and start no fight cooldown.
- The board shows the host's bug, level, condition, gear and combat setup before joining. The host's fighter is captured when posted; the joining bug is captured when joining. Care or gear changes do not change an already-posted fighter, except its health and packed lunch, which are read when the fight starts. Cancel before changing its combat setup or joining a different bout.
- Held stakes remain part of net worth. Cancel an open bout before deleting the account.
- Signed-in players can see, for every bug in the ring, its name, species, level, looks, record, whether it has fainted, and `inRing` when that owner is already in a fight; and for each recent fight, the bugs, their owners, the blows, the result and the pot. Wallets, payout receipts, combat setups, condition, needs, XP and care history remain private.

## The circuit

The Barn's Circuit room is the single-player campaign (`server/circuit.js`, `client/src/barn/Circuit.jsx`). Each area is a fixed path of stops; a stop opens once every stop before it is done, except the Bug Center, which is always open. Eight areas and the Fairgrounds exist. The Back Forty (ribbon 1) and the Culvert (ribbon 2) come in that order. Ribbon 2 then opens four at once, to be won in any order: the Front Porch (3), Buffalo Bill's Back Kitchen (4), the Van at the Mine Entrance (5) and Shaft 12, Night Shift (6). Each of those names the ribbon it `needs`. The last two come in order and name how many ribbons the player must hold (`held`): the Starlite Drive-In (7) with six, and the Old Rail Spur (8) with seven. Their levels are fixed, a level or so around the cap the player has by then.
- **The Fairgrounds** open with all eight ribbons (`held: 8`; the area's `ribbon: 9` is only its purse multiplier). The Nephew is at the gate, then four `judge` stops in a row, each a three-bug gauntlet drilled harder than the rest of the circuit (22%, `BOARD`) that pays `PURSE.judge` and no ribbon: Marjorie (encore: the move your bug used last does 20% less), Hollis (inspect: your bug's hits run from 85% at mood 0 to 105% at mood 100), Gunt (best in show: your bug takes 8% more for each empty gear slot) and Korn (pull: his bugs hit 50% harder and are 18 points likelier to miss). Beating Korn lifts the level cap from 18 to 20.
- **The Grand Champion** is the `champion` stop. Whoever last won it holds the rosette, and a copy of the bug that won it (species, name, level and stance as they were) sits there, drilled like a judge's bug and fought by the computer; it is `champion` in `circuit.json`. Until anyone wins, the house holds it (`HOUSE`). The holder cannot fight their own bug. The stop is never done: lose the rosette and you can come back for it. Its purse is paid the first time only.
- **The last two leaders.** Reel Ray's rule is rewind: once per bug, the first time it is left standing under a quarter of its health, it goes back to 45% (`RULES.rewind`). Off Track Jack's is retake: his bug takes a missed attack again, twice a fight (`RULES.retake`). Jack's first bug is Steeze's.
- **Any-order areas scale.** Every bug in them is `SCALE` (1.5) levels higher, rounded, for each ribbon the player holds past two, and a leader's third bug (`from: 4`) only turns up once the player holds four. A gauntlet under way keeps the lineup it began with. Their purses are those of the next ribbon the player would win (ribbons held plus one), whichever slot it fills.

- **Circuit bugs are drilled.** They fight in top condition with the best gear, and with 10% more health and damage than a barn bug of their level (`TRAINED`, `trained` on the fighter). Left to itself, a barn bug of the same level wins about one fight in eight, and one a level below about one in twelve.
- **Purses** are one-time prize money, bigger each area: a trainer pays $75 and a ribbon $750, times the area's ribbon number (`PURSE`). A trainer with more than one bug is a gauntlet that pays once at the end. They are `circuit` entries in the transaction log.
- **Fights** are ordinary ring fights (`kind: 'circuit'`, followed with `GET /api/barn/fight` like a Barn bout) against bugs nobody owns. No stake. A win pays the mine's battle XP (20 plus the upset bonus); a loss pays nothing; a knockout sends the bug to the Bug Center as usual (`faintedIn: 'circuit'`). A beaten trainer stays beaten. A draw counts as a loss.
- **Leaders** field their bugs one after another. Each round you send in any standing bug that has not lost a round of this gauntlet; its health carries into its next round, and so does the leader's bug's. Beat them all for the area's ribbon and `RIBBON_XP` (75) for every bug that won a round. Fighting another stop, or Walk away, drops the gauntlet, and it starts from the first bug next time.
- **Feature presentations.** Each area ends on a `boss` stop, open once that area's ribbon is won and never needed to go on: Charlene (the Back Forty), THOSE! (the Culvert), Jimmy Conscience (the Front Porch), the Roach Choir (the Back Kitchen), Dr. Brindle (the Van), General Mandibull (Shaft 12), MOTHERA (the Drive-In, reusing the porch-light rule) and Barry B. Bensonhurst (the Rail Spur, reusing the web). They are one of a kind and cannot be owned. Beating one pays `PURSE.boss` ($300, times the area's multiplier) and wins its card, saved as `cards` in `circuit.json` and listed in the circuit view. A boss with several bugs is a gauntlet like a leader's. Their powers are house rules: web (the other bug's hits do 12% less and are 5 points likelier to miss), conscience (it heals 7 whenever the other bug uses poison or a pierce), sing (it heals 3 at the start of each own turn), telepod (once, under 35% health, the two bugs trade how hurt they are). THOSE! is simply drilled much harder (`trained: 0.4`).
- **Rare wild stops.** Each area has a `wild` stop with one rare bug (existing species: the ironclad beetle, sea skater, glowworm, Asian bombardier, black beauty stick insect and thorntree cicada; the last two areas hold uncommons, the sabertooth longhorn and the railroad worm). It is not drilled, pays no purse, and nothing waits on it. Knock it out and the mine's catching rules apply (`WILD`, `jarChance`, `caughtLevel` in `server/pvp.js`): three throws with `POST /api/circuit/jar { fight }` while that fight is still on the books (about a minute). Caught, it takes a stall and the stop is done for good; otherwise it can be fought again. The catch in progress is `catch` in `circuit.json`.
- **Errands.** A `quest` stop is somebody wanting something (`QUESTS` in `server/circuit.js` holds each check): P.Q. Flea wants a Flier of level 6 or more to model for a fake bird, Nurse Hikari wants to borrow a bug that glows, and Squanto wants to see three feature-presentation cards. Nothing is taken. `POST /api/circuit/quest { stop }` marks it done and pays its reward once; nothing waits on an errand.
- **For the wall.** The first ribbon and the first feature-presentation card each put an award in the player's My House inventory (`AWARDS`: `odd-ribbonrack`, `odd-bugposter`), the way the golf medal arrives. Neither is for sale. Players who won theirs before this existed get them the next time they open the circuit.
- **Prizes.** Each leader hands over a prize with the ribbon (`PRIZES`): the Whistle (first attack cannot miss), Floaties (the flood and the porch light do nothing), the Night Light (Dwayne's dark shaft hides nothing), the Oven Mitt (Fire bugs' hits do 10% less), the Antidote (poison does half damage and always wears off) Night Eyes (an Ambush bug's first strike can miss), Bottomless Popcorn (2 HP back at the start of each own turn) and the Retake (one missed attack taken again per fight). A prize is held by having beaten its leader, nothing is stored for it, and it rides on the player's bug as `prizes` in circuit fights only.
- **The rival.** The Nephew's stop fields one bug, Bug Two: a common species of a type that beats the player's partner, chosen the first time the player looks and kept after that (`rivalSpecies`, saved as `rival` in `circuit.json`).
- **Tolls.** A toll stop (Chopper & the Hoppers) can be fought like a trainer, for the purse, or paid with `POST /api/circuit/toll { stop }`, which costs the toll and pays nothing. Their rule is tribute: a packed lunch on the player's bug is taken at the start of the fight and their bug eats it instead.
- **House rules** ride on the leader's bugs (`rule` in `server/engine/bout.js`). Bent's cough: before any action either bug has a 12% chance to lose the turn. Dale's flicker: every third overall turn the porch light is off and an attack from anything but a Dazzle bug is 22 points likelier to miss. Ruperto's grill: his bugs' Fire flare-up is on at any health, and every fourth own turn hits 30% harder. Aldine Voss's linger: poison her bugs put on never wears off. Dwayne's dark: the fight is unchanged, but the fight view carries `dark: true` and the page shows neither his bug's health nor who is favoured while it is live (the numbers are still in the response). Tcut's rage: below half health his bug hits 25% harder. Kashely's mulligan: his bug takes up to three missed attacks again. Gil's flood: from overall turn 9, every bug that is not a Water type loses 3 HP at the start of each of its own turns, and his tell shows as the water comes up. Coach Pugh's dodgeball: every third own turn her bug's attack cannot miss and hits 25% harder, and the ring shows her tell (`tell` in the fight view) the turn before.
- **Persistence:** `circuit.json` holds each player's finished stops, ribbons, the Commissioner's answer and any gauntlet in progress. The fight itself is memory only: a restart mid-round calls that round off.

## The Bug Gym

`POST /api/barn/gym { path, bug? }` runs one session for a hatched, standing bug (not fainted, sick, taken, held by the Heeshee, or while one of the owner's bugs is fighting). The rules live in `server/engine/gym.js`; the answer is the barn view plus `gym: { path, outcome, xp, cost, delta, change, before, after }`.

| Path | Cooldown | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cardio & Drills (`clean`) | 30 min | fit +0.01, mood +6, fed/clean −4, strain −5, 9 XP |
| Sparring Pit (`spar`) | 45 min | fed −8, clean −10; 60% a sharp bout (fit +0.02, mood −2, 14 XP), else a rough scrap (fit +0.01, mood −6, 8 XP) |
| Double Shift (`overwork`) | 15 min | fit +0.035, mood −20, fed −12, clean −10, strain +30, 12 XP; refused at strain 90+ |
| Back-Alley Roids (`roids`) | 60 min | $25 (`barn-gym` ledger entry), mood −40, roid load +40 (max 100) |
| Cool Down & Stretch (`cooldown`) | 30 min | roid −20, strain −35, mood +4 |

Each bug may take one path per calendar day in `America/Los_Angeles`. The stamp is `pen.gymLastAt`; a bug with none may train right away. The lock lifts at the next Pacific midnight after that workout (DST-correct: the day is an Intl timezone key, not a fixed UTC offset). Creatine shortens that lock to 12 hours from the workout, or to midnight PT if midnight comes first. Existing stamps need no migration: a workout from a previous Pacific day is already spent for that day and ready now, and a workout from today stays locked until midnight (or the creatine 12-hour mark). Per-path cooldowns still apply and can outlast the daily lock when a bug trains close to midnight.

Every path but roids and cool-down is refused below 10 Fed. `pen.gym` holds `fit` (0 to 0.2, kept for good), `roid` and `strain` (0 to 100; they fade 0.75 and 1.5 points an hour from `at`), per-path `cool` stamps, `sessions` and `last`. A bug without `gym` reads as all zeros, so older `barn.json` rows need nothing. From those: damage `fit + 0.006 × roid` (at most +80%), health `0.002 × roid` (at most +20%), unruly `0.7 × roid + 0.5 × strain` (0 to 100), mine pace `1 − 0.006 × unruly` (at least 40%), balk chance `unruly / 200` every five seconds of a guide's walking, first-strike penalty `0.15 × unruly / 100`, and roid rage `0.006 × roid + 0.004 × max(0, strain − 50)` (at most 85%, zero for a clean bug).

A fighter snapshot carries `gym`, `bulk` and `slow` only when they are not zero (`engine/bout.js` multiplies damage and health by them and shifts who goes first), so an untrained bug's fights and lines are unchanged. In the Acid Mines, `guideBug` hands the page the guide's pace, balk and rage, and `Pvp.startWild` records the rage chance. When the partner knocks out a wild bug, one roll on its own stream (`rageInt`, out of 10,000) decides whether it goes berserk: the wild bug leaves the shift, `over.rage` is set, there is no jar phase and `/mines/pvp/jar` refuses. `GYM_TEST_RAGE=1` (dev login only) makes every roll a rage for screenshots.

## Sound

A fight's effects and music follow the Barn's one sound switch, which is off until a player turns it on, and stop at once when it is turned off. Both are made in the browser; there are no audio files. The music plays from the first blow to the finish. Each blow is followed by a short pause so its line can be read before the next one lands.

## Fight invariants and compatibility

- Both bugs must be hatched, alive, not fainted and not already in a fight when joining. Any two levels may fight; the board shows the difference before players commit.
- A fight ends at knockout or 32 alternating actions. Surviving bugs compare remaining HP percentages; equal percentages draw. Venom events and a packed lunch can make a replay longer than 32 events.
- Existing bugs retain previously learned moves from the old level-1/3/5 schedule, even before the revised unlock level. The two-special limit still applies.
- Old replays retain their original 100-HP presentation and earn no retroactive XP. New replays include maximum HP, moves, the XP award and before/after levels. A participant's own replay may show the opponent's stance and equipped moves; the public replay carries only the bugs as drawn, their owners, the blows and the pot. Room visits include no battle setup, replay or private care history.

Where a body takes `bug`, it is one of the caller's bugs by its `id` from `stable.bugs`; left out, it means the partner. An id that is not theirs: 404.

Example setup, once those moves are learned:

```json
{ "stance": "balanced" }
```

Move IDs are `basic`, `technique`, `signature`, `tactic` and `capstone`. The response is the updated Barn view. Older saves that chose two specials keep their stance; the choice of specials is ignored.

`barn.json` retains the pet's optional `battle`, `combatVersion` and `legacyAbilities` fields and `lastBout`. Version-2 bouts include `maxHp`, `xpEarned`, `levelBefore` and `levelAfter`. Version-3 bouts add the bout ID, stake, payout and net result, with rounds oriented to each participant; newer ones also name both owners. Version-5 bouts were fought live; their rounds may include a lost turn (`skipped`), a bombardier's pop (`pop`) or a packed lunch being eaten (`type: "snack"`). Since stakes follow the line a receipt also holds `line` (that bug's chance) and `against` (the other corner's stake), and a recorded fight its `pot`; older ones have neither and read as an even $`stake × 2` pot. Old replays still work. A bug's packed lunch is `lunch` (`name`, `heal`) on its own entry, removed when a fight in which it was eaten ends.

`barn.json` keeps the partner in `pens`, stalled bugs in `bench` and bought stalls in `stalls`, all by owner; a stalled bug carries `benchedAt`, the moment its slow clock started. Saves from before stalls need no change: their one bug is the partner.

`barn.json` also holds `challenges`, which carry no money, and `fights`, the ring's record of the latest 20 bouts. A fight is recorded in the same save as the first pet reward for it, so it needs no write of its own and is recovered with that reward after a failed write or restart. Bouts fought before this record existed are not in it.

**Battle record** (`server/battles.js`, the leaderboard's Bug battles tab). `battles.json` keeps every finished bug fight: Barn bouts (`bout`), wild bugs (`wild`) and miner-on-miner battles (`raid`) in the mine, and the Bug Circuit (`circuit`). Each entry holds both corners (owner, bug name, species, level), the winner side (`null` for a draw; a circuit draw is logged as a loss), why it ended when someone walked off, and each side's XP and cash. It is logged by the code that settles the fight (Barn `settle`, Pvp `finish`, Circuit `settle`), once per fight id, and never moves money. An abandoned mine battle (both miners gone) is not logged. The newest 2,000 entries are kept; per-player tallies live in `counts`, so totals never shrink. The first boot with this file backfills: `base` freezes each player's older totals (bout W/L/D from the ring records of every bug kept, including stalls and the farm; mine-battle W/L from `pvp.json` battle raids; wild W/L from the bugs' `mineRecord` minus the raids that counted toward it; the circuit starts at zero because nothing counted it), and the Barn's last 20 `fights` and every battle raid still in `pvp.json` become entries with details. Graves keep no record, and a traded bug's record went with the bug. `GET /api/battles` (anyone) returns per-player totals; `GET /api/battles/:id` (signed in) adds the fights. Losing the file loses only the history; delete it and the next boot backfills again from what is left.

`barn.json` holds the Bugdex in `dex`: by owner, the entries met and when. An entry is a species id for the bug as hatched, or `species:1` and `species:2` for its evolved stages; each is met on its own, so fighting a stage three bug reveals neither of the others. A player meets an entry by keeping that bug (marked whenever the Barn brings it up to date, with every stage it came through), by being an owner in any fight that starts in `server/fights.js` (ring, circuit or mine, both bugs, each in the stage its `style.evo` gives), or by a mine sighting (`POST /api/mines/pvp/saw`, see [Acid Mines](acid-mines.md)). Watching a replay or reading the ring's roster does not count. A fight's start marks in memory only, so that it adds no write to the stake commit; the fight's own ending saves it. Saves from before the Bugdex are filled in at start-up from bugs kept, graves, the recorded fights and finished circuit stops (a paid toll was never fought). Deleting an account removes its entries.

`players.json` stores `barnBouts.open` with the held stake and fighter snapshot. On joining, both stakes move into `barnBouts.live` in one write and the fight starts; the fight itself lives in memory (`server/fights.js`). A `live` entry whose fight is gone, as after a restart, is refunded the next time the Barn checks. When the fight ends, both wallets, transaction logs and `barnBouts.lastBout` reward receipts commit together through `commitPlayerDrafts`. Only then does each pet receive XP, its record entry, cooldown and replay. `barn.json` saves `settledBoutId` with those changes; refreshing after a failed pet write or restart applies a pending receipt exactly once. The receipt is tied to that bug's birth time and is not applied to a replacement pet. A player must catch up before entering another fight, so the latest receipt suffices. A failed wallet write leaves the listing and wallets unchanged. Preserve **both files** for backup/restore; do not roll back to code that ignores held stakes or reward receipts.

**Trades** (`server/trades.js`, the Barn's Trade room). A trade names the proposer's bugs (`give`), the recipient's (`want`) and optional cash from the proposer (`money`); a cash offer has no `give`. Offers live in `barn.json` `trades` (open ones, plus closed ones for 14 days) and expire after 48 hours. Cash leaves the proposer's bankroll when the offer is made, in the same `players.json` write that records it under `barnTrades.held[tradeId]`; it counts toward net worth through `Barn.escrow`. Accepting moves every bug and flips the trade to `accepted` in one `barn.json` write, then pays the held cash to the recipient in one `players.json` write. Declined, cancelled, expired and called-off offers return it. A held entry is the only marker: on any Barn look, an entry whose trade was accepted is paid on, and any other entry whose trade is no longer open is returned, so a crash between the two writes is finished once. Ownership is checked again at accept time; a traded bug arrives on the slow stall clock (or as partner in an empty barn), renumbered if its id clashes with one the receiver keeps, with the receiver's Bugdex marked. Worn store gear stays with the old owner. Preserve both files here too.
